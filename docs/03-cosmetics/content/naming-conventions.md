# Naming Conventions

Naming feels trivial at 66 items and becomes a real problem at 500. Set the rules now.

## Gear names are literal

Gear is named for what it is: "Roll-top pack," "Platform boots," "Trench coat." Players need to search and filter by garment type, and a player who wants a hoodie should be able to find the hoodie.

The exception is Legendary gear, which may be named evocatively — "Anti-grav boots," "Star-catching net" — because at that tier the item is a specific object rather than a category.

## Skin names are evocative and never colour-literal

Skins get two-word names with a texture or place quality: "Void Weave," "Chrome Dip," "Chalk," "Tidepool."

Avoid naming a skin after its colour. "Blue Skin" ages badly the moment you ship a second blue one, and it gives you nowhere to go. Name the *material* or the *feeling*.

## One word is a luxury, reserve it

Single-word names read as more premium — "Chalk," "Ink," "Orrery." Use them sparingly and deliberately, weighted toward higher tiers, or the effect wears off.

## Never reuse a name across axes

No skin should share a name with a garment. A player reading "you received Orrery" must know immediately whether they got the staff or the skin.

## Prefixes for variants, not new names

A prismatic Void Weave is "Prismatic Void Weave," not a new name. Variants must be recognisable as variants of their parent in a list, or the dex becomes unreadable.

## Reserve a naming space for Mythics

Mythics should feel categorically different. One workable rule: Mythics get a definite article — "The Long Night," "The Cartographer." Nothing else in the catalogue is allowed one.
