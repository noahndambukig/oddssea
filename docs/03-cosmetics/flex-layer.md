# The Flex Layer

Avatars have a structural problem: **only six items are visible at once.** A player with 200 items is displaying 3% of their collection. Without somewhere to put the rest, deep collectors stop buying the moment their six slots are filled — precisely the failure this system exists to prevent.

## Required

**The Closet.** A browsable, visitable space showing everything a player owns, organised by slot and tier, with completed sets on mannequins. Reachable from their profile.

**Loadout presets.** At least three saved outfits, all displayed on the profile. Players build looks; let them keep and show more than one.

**Combined rarity readout.** Show how few players share a given gear + skin pairing — "this combination appears on 14 avatars." This surfaces combination scarcity, which emerges free from the two axes without authoring anything new, and it is the deepest flex available to a thoughtful collector.

**Comparison view.** When visiting a profile, show what they own that you do not. Slightly ruthless; extremely effective.

## Later

**Visitor tipping.** Let admirers tip **Shells** on a closet or outfit (`decisions/0016`). Social approval feeds bankroll, which feeds wagering, which feeds Pearls — the tip enters the loop at the top rather than skipping it. A per-player daily cap on tips *received* is what stops reciprocal farming rings; amounts in `02-economy/currency-model.md`.

**Fusion.** Consume four items of one tier plus currency for one item of the next tier up. Genuinely deflationary — it destroys items *and* currency — while delivering the gambling thrill. Denominations land in the `02-economy/currency-model.md` rewrite.

**Wear / condition.** A cheap third axis: patina, sun-fading, creasing. One overlay shader, large perceived depth, and an entire secondary market. CS:GO's float value is essentially this.

**Player marketplace.** With a burn on every sale — see `02-economy/guardrails.md`.
