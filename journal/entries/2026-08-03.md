## 13:36 — decision — Economy revamp: drop dust, make the second currency wager-earned

**What happened:** Noah decided to revamp the economy around wagering as the
core activity, replacing the dust design. New structure: Currency 1 is earned
via tasks and is the only wagerable currency; bets pay back odds × stake in
Currency 1 as standard betting. Currency 2 is earned *exclusively* through
wagering — "a lot for winning bets, a little for losing ones" — and is what
buys crates and cosmetics. Stated intent: "The whole point is to reward
wagering."

This reverses `docs/decisions/0004-dust-is-a-closed-loop.md` in effect —
dust's closed loop existed to reward collection depth without touching the
coin economy; Currency 2's job is instead to reward gambling volume. It also
resolves the mismatch where the currency model's faucets were written for a
flat-payout match game while the project is a gambling simulator.

Direction is decided; the mechanics are not. Open at time of writing: how C2
rewards scale (flat per bet vs stake-proportional vs odds-scaled), what
inherits dust's jobs (duplicate salvage, set stipends), bankroll-ruin
protection, marketplace denomination, and all actual numbers.

**Why it matters:** Invalidates most of `docs/02-economy/` — currency-model
faucets/sinks, `dust.md` entirely, guardrail metrics, and both simulations.
The compliance rewrite (roadmap Q2/Q5) becomes coupled to this: wagerable C1
plus crates makes "is C1 ever purchasable" the load-bearing regulatory
question. Cosmetics specs (`03-cosmetics/`) survive with sinks re-denominated
to C2.

**Refs:** `docs/decisions/0004-dust-is-a-closed-loop.md`,
`docs/02-economy/currency-model.md`, `docs/02-economy/dust.md`,
`docs/00-project/roadmap.md`, commit `3b1b918`

## 13:46 — decision — Set completion pays a one-time C1 bonus, not a stipend

**What happened:** Resolved the open question from the 13:36 entry about what
inherits dust's jobs. Noah initially wanted set completion to pay uncapped
per-day C1 — "the whole point is that they should be rewarded for completing
the sets so they can gamble more." Argued against: a per-day stipend on a
monotonic counter is a perpetuity that compounds through the core loop (sets →
C1/day → handle → C2 → crates → sets). At an illustrative 100 C1/day per set,
5 completed sets already out-earn the committed task faucet, and ~20 sets
would generate ~5× a new committed player's C2 income — breaking faucet-based
tuning, new-content pacing, flat-balance metrics, and quietly opening a
permanent C2→C1 channel.

Settled: **completion pays a one-time C1 bonus** — no per-day component.
One-time payouts can be generous because they pay per completion event, not
forever. Ongoing veteran rewards (free-play stipends scoped to active-season
sets were sketched as the likely shape) are explicitly **deferred, not
rejected** — out of scope for the current revamp.

Also confirmed this session: duplicate salvage pays lossy C2, marketplace
trades in C2 only (a C1 marketplace would let crate luck convert to bankroll),
and the C2 reward formula is odds-scaled and stake-proportional (win ∝ stake ×
odds, loss consolation ∝ stake) to kill favorite-grinding as a dominant
strategy.

**Why it matters:** The two-currency revamp's structure is now fully settled;
what remains is numbers (house edge, C2 reward coefficients, task faucet
sizes, crate prices in C2, comeback-floor size, completion bonus size) and the
rewrite of `docs/02-economy/` plus new simulations (bankroll-ruin replaces
set-completion as the core sim). The one-time bonus is a bounded C2→C1
conversion, so its size must be derived with the other numbers, not chosen
independently.

**Refs:** 13:36 entry above, `docs/02-economy/dust.md`,
`docs/02-economy/guardrails.md`, commit `3b1b918`

## 14:52 — decision — Web-first PWA on web-native TypeScript, hosted on AWS

**What happened:** Platform, client stack and hosting settled in one
discussion. v1 is a responsive web app installable as a PWA; no native
builds or store submission until after the compliance rewrite. Client is
TypeScript + a component framework (React vs Svelte deferred — roadmap
question 10) + PixiJS for WebGL surfaces. Hosting is AWS (Noah has

(~$0–10/mo dev, ~$40–90/mo soft launch, ~$200–500/mo at ~50k MAU) —
deliberately not recorded in specs because they drift.

Specced as `docs/04-technical/platform.md` and `hosting.md` (both agreed),
decision entry 0006. Two rules baked in as non-negotiable from the first
line of code: mobile-first as a completion gate, and a server-authoritative
economy with ledger-style currency mutations and logged crate opens (the
audit trail behind the published-odds commitment in compliance).

Also found and fixed while filing: the docs tree had production files at
`docs/04-production/` while the README map said `05-production/` and

**Why it matters:** First technical specs in the repo — `04-technical/` is

0007 as a shipping condition for blackjack; formula refinement lands with

## 21:02 — finding — First wager-economy simulations hit every anchor

**What happened:** Built and ran the new simulation suite
(`simulations/bankroll.py`, `simulations/crate-game.py`) under the 0010
constants. En route, two decisions: Noah resolved the first-Legendary
tension by **volume instead of a newbie guarantee** — 50 basic crates/week
for dedicated players at a 1% Legendary rate (`decisions/0010`) — and
added a **premium crate tier** (boosted Epic/Legendary odds, higher
price). The considered-and-dropped newbie guarantee is recorded in 0010.

Candidate constants: task income 500/day committed (7 days), 350/day
casual (4 days); min bet 10 (⇒ comeback floor 35 min bets, exactly Noah's
"like 35"); edges races 10% / instant 3% / roulette 2.7% / blackjack 1.5%;
Pearl formula 0.75·stake·edge base + 0.30·stake·edge·odds on wins; basic
crate 70 Pearls (Legendary 1%, pity 200), premium 280 (Legendary 5%, pity
40); set crate 90 (keystone 2%, pity 100).

Results: committed typical-bettor ≈ **47 crates/wk** (target ~50), casual
≈ **20** (target ~20). First Legendary median **1.3 wk / 3.2 wk**
(committed/casual), p90 3.8 / 9.5 wk — the casual p90 is exactly the
200-crate pity bound doing its job. **Pity fires on 13% of chases** — felt,
as intended. ~31 / ~12 Legendaries per year. Busting is self-inflicted:
zero busts for cautious/typical archetypes, ~0.4/wk aggressive, daily for
all-in — who still earn Pearls, just fewer. Set chase (30% of Pearl income
to set crates): median **3 wk committed / 7 wk casual** (p90 9 / 21) —
strikingly close to the old economy's ~22/~47-day targets, now emergent
rather than assumed.

**Why it matters:** The wager economy works at the first full-system
check — the volume-not-guarantee resolution produces a fast honeymoon, a
felt pity, and a defensible Legendary flow simultaneously. The candidate
constants are ready to become the rewritten `currency-model.md` pending
Noah's approval. Premium-tier pricing (280/5%) is designed but only
shallowly validated; worth a dedicated look in the rewrite.

**Refs:** `docs/02-economy/simulations/bankroll.py`,
`docs/02-economy/simulations/crate-game.py`,
