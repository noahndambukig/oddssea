## 00:20 — decision — Game modes drafted; the lottery is deliberately positive-EV

**What happened:** The game-modes session ran talk-then-write in one
sitting. Settled via pickers: **persistent race roster** (~14 named sea
creatures with visible form; odds always derived from hidden drifting

**win-only race bets** at launch, **no maximum bet in v1** (all-in is a
modeled archetype; the comeback floor is the safety net), and the
headline: Noah's lottery design — **pot = 1.5× ticket sales, a free 50%

The match makes the lottery the only positive-EV game in the house —
deliberately, as a communal generosity moment under the countdown.
Unbounded it is a dominant strategy and an infinite money printer, so
three bounds went in with it: per-player ticket caps sized so the max
expected subsidy ≈ one daily task (~75 Shells/day), no Pearls from
tickets (theo is undefined at negative edge), and the match accounted as
a second, capped Shell faucet — guardrails rule 1 amended from "one
faucet, one sink" to "two faucets, one sink."

`01-game/game-modes.md` drafted (all seven games, disclosure-everywhere
rule, atomic settlement); lottery figures added to `currency-model.md`
marked provisional pending a bankroll-sim lottery term (new roadmap
question 11). Housekeeping: roadmap Q6 (economy numbers) finally struck
— it had been resolved yesterday but never struck.

**Why it matters:** Every launch game now has a written spec; the last
big design gap after this is compliance and the data model. The
positive-EV lottery is the design's most unusual economic object — the
first mechanic whose safety depends on a cap rather than an edge, and
the bankroll sim must confirm the destruction ratio holds before its
numbers lock.

committed maxes caps for +775 Shells/wk, casual ~+225) and re-ran. The

## 11:13 — decision — Compliance rewritten around one wall: no real money, ever, in v1

**What happened:** `06-risks/compliance.md` rewritten for the settled
genre (it had predated the gambling-simulator decision). The organising
idea: a single load-bearing wall — **no real-money path in any
direction**. Shells earned only, Pearls wager-earned only, nothing
purchasable, nothing cashes out. That wall is what keeps a gambling
simulator outside gambling regulation (no consideration, no prize of
value) and outside the *Big Fish Casino* line of social-casino cases,
which all turned on *purchased* chips. Resolves roadmap question 5 for
v1: Shells are never purchasable; any post-v1 change requires a decision
entry and legal advice, in that order.

The rest of the posture: self-imposed 18+ age attestation at signup from
day one (matching the 17+/18+ store ratings the genre gets anyway);
disclose-everything on odds even though unpaid crates don't legally
require it; trading named as "the leak in the wall" (off-platform RMT is
how skins-gambling scandals start — late shipping, burn, tier
restrictions, no cash-out, and a compliance lean added to roadmap Q7
that Mythics should be untradeable); and an honest inventory of our own
risky patterns — the loss-rebate Pearls, the +EV lottery, referral
incentives, habit mechanics — each named with why it is safe behind the
wall. Old doc's operational risks carried forward, plus lottery pot
integrity as a new headline risk.

**Why it matters:** The last spec that predated the genre decision is
gone; every doc in the tree now describes the same game. Compliance is
no longer a "needs revisiting" flag but an agreed posture with one
tripwire: the wall comes down only with legal review. Remaining pre-code
work: the data model, rosters-to-JSON, the racer content pass, and
draft reviews.

**Refs:** `docs/06-risks/compliance.md`, `docs/00-project/roadmap.md`,

## 11:32 — finding — GitHub's ID-embedded OIDC sub claims broke the standard trust recipe

**What happened:** The first pipeline deploy failed at the credentials step:
`Not authorized to perform sts:AssumeRoleWithWebIdentity`, 12 retries. The
role's trust condition required the classic subject
`repo:noahndambukig/oddssea:ref:refs/heads/main`; CloudTrail's record of the
rejected call showed the token actually carried
`repo:noahndambukig@210443285/oddssea@1317725621:ref:refs/heads/main` —
GitHub now embeds the immutable user and repo IDs in the `sub` claim, and
`StringEquals` correctly refused the mismatch. The name-only recipe that
virtually every OIDC tutorial teaches no longer matches this repo's tokens.
The reason for GitHub's change is sound: names are re-registrable, so
name-based trust is open to resurrection attacks (delete or rename, attacker
re-registers the name, inherits the trust); numeric IDs cannot be reused.
Fix: the trust condition now accepts both forms via an IAM condition-value
array (OR semantics), with ownerId/repoId pinned in `infra/lib/config.ts`.
Diagnosis path worth remembering: CloudTrail → Event history →
AssumeRoleWithWebIdentity shows the exact rejected `sub` — no guessing.

**Why it matters:** Anyone reproducing this repo's CI/CD from the walkthrough
would have hit the same wall; the failure-mode table now covers it. The
ID-pinned form is also strictly better security than what was originally
designed — the incident upgraded the trust policy. Increment A's
pipeline-proof step is still pending: the fixed Cicd stack must be
redeployed (manual by design) and the workflow re-run.

**Refs:** `infra/lib/cicd-stack.ts`, `infra/lib/config.ts`,
`infra/README.md` failure-modes table, commit `141ee99` (pre-fix)
