# 2026-08-08

## 01:35 — decision — Crash planned: rounds are arithmetic, and two review rounds moved real ground

**What happened:** The crash milestone was planned and approved. Four
design forks went to pickers: **time-indexed lazy rounds** (round = the
UTC minute; bust = HMAC(secret, round index) through the edge law; no
scheduler, settlement written lazily on player touch) over a sweeper
cron and over ticker-owned round rows; **both cash-out verbs** (live
press adjudicated by the server clock + auto-target); **derivation
documented, commit-reveal ceremony deferred**; **no bet-function
extraction** — crash's two-phase lifecycle (debit now, settle later) is
a new shape, not a third copy of the instant shape, so the rule-of-three
extraction waits for roulette. The bust law came out clean: with
`P(B ≥ m) = 0.97/m`, every 2-decimal target ≥ 1.01× has an identical
97.00% multiplier RTP — no table to tune, two constants and a formula.
002's schema turned out to have been waiting since the ledger milestone:
`state`, nullable `settled_at`, and `decimal_odds` ("nullable on
purpose: crash has no price until the player cashes out").

**Why it matters:** Codex review ran twice and both rounds found
blockers in *my* design, not cosmetics. Round 1 (7 findings): the UI
animated the curve blind — the client could keep offering cashout after
the round had busted; fixed with a ~1 s flight poll where the server
reveals the bust the moment `t_bust` passes (time-gated, not
trust-gated — the past is public). Round 2 (5 findings): my "matured =
minute over" settlement condition imposed up to 46 s of artificial lag —
a round is *decided* at `t_bust`, nothing after it can change any
outcome, so eligibility became decided-not-elapsed; integer Shell
flooring silently broke the 97.00% claim at small stakes (stake 10 at
1.09× pays 10 — an 89% effective RTP), and Noah made the design call to
**keep min stake 10 and publish the floor** rather than force stakes
into multiples of 100; bust ties resolved differently across the two
verbs (auto won, manual lost — ~0.94% of cases at 1.01×), settled as
**ties pay in both**, matching the published `≥` law. Also caught:
a keyed place retry short-circuits at its stored idempotent response,
so skipped-round follow-ups must use the keyless settle; and
`numeric(10,2)` silently rounds off-grid targets like 1.019, so SQL
rejects the cent grid violation instead. Twelve findings applied across
two rounds; the defect trend argument for pre-build review keeps
compounding.

## 02:25 — milestone — Crash is live and verified; two execution defects, both in the handler's plumbing

**What happened:** The crash milestone deployed and verified in
production. Pre-deploy: typecheck, build, credential-free synth,
migration splitter clean on 012 (12 statements, 6 intact function
bodies), and a 40/40 harness whose tail checks are ANALYTIC — Codex
R1#7 killed the flaky 1M-sample tail test, and `m·P(B ≥ m)` is instead
computed exactly as a floor count over the 48-bit grid at every target.
The production suite gained a new power: running with admin
credentials, it reads the round secret and PLANS — it computed every
upcoming bust and assigned each scenario a qualifying round (a bust ≥ 3
for the live-win press, ≤ 1.5 for the late press, an auto-target set
EXACTLY equal to a future round's bust to prove ties pay, a ≥ 1.09
round for the stake-10 floor case, and an abandoned straggler). Final
matrix: 44 distinct checks green across two passes, plus a browser
smoke (flight phase renders, the multiplier ticks between polls, the
disclosure shows the law, the Shell-floor sentence, the HMAC recipe and
97.00%).

**Why it matters:** After three zero-defect milestones, the suite found
TWO defects — both in the handler's plumbing, neither in SQL or the
economics. First: `callFunction` builds calls positionally from object
insertion order, and a `...spread` of shared timing params appended
them at the END while the SQL signatures declare them mid-list — the
call resolved to a function that does not exist (the crates-era
overload trap in positional form; settle alone worked because its
declared tail happens to match the spread). Second: the handler maps
SQL rule rejections to clean 400s via a MESSAGE ALLOW-LIST regex, and
none of crash's new rejection phrases were registered — real rules
("already riding this round", "target must be on the cent grid",
"betting window closed") surfaced as generic `request_failed`. Both are
the same genus: a convention (parameter order, message registration)
that nothing enforces structurally. The defect trend across eight
verified deployments now reads 10 → 1 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 2, and
both 2s lived in the seam between Node and SQL — exactly where the
walkthrough keeps finding its lessons. Everything the two Codex rounds
hardened (ties, eligibility, the empty-map race, the reveal gate, the
floor) passed first try.

## 03:05 — decision — Roulette planned: one formula prices the whole table, and Codex caught a real exploit window

**What happened:** The roulette milestone was planned and approved.
Pickers: **full standard bet matrix** (all ten types now — adjacency
derived from the 3×12 grid, never typed in), **the rule-of-three
extraction, forward-only** (a private `settle_round_bet` ledger core
for roulette now and races later; dice/plinko/crash untouched), **40 s
rounds per the spec** (30 s betting, spin at :30). The design's
pleasant discoveries: every European payout is `36/coverage` — one
identity prices the entire table, the RTP is exactly 36/37 at every
type and stake, and the whole-Shell floor caveat crash needed simply
does not exist (integer payouts). The pocket is HMAC-derived with
**deterministic rejection** instead of `mod 37` — naked modulo carries
bias, rejection makes the published 1/37 exact. One retained secret
serves both games via domain separation ('roulette:' prefix).

**Why it matters:** Codex round 1 (7 findings, all applied) caught a
genuine exploit window my plan carried: the round view revealed the
pocket by **Lambda's clock** while SQL closed betting by **its own** —
at the :30 boundary, clock skew could reveal the pocket while bets
were still accepted. Unlike crash (an early bust reveal is only
avoidance information), a revealed pocket is directly bankable at
36×. The fix makes Postgres's `now()` the one authority for reveal,
phase and the client's clock sync. Also applied: chips carry their
selection (the layout needs positions); the settle response window
became TIME-based (`settled_at` within 5 min) because with many bets
per round a round-count window both unbounds and reopens the
old-straggler replay gap; settlement order pinned to
`round_index, bet_id` (UUIDv7 = creation order) so the pearl
fraction-carry is audit-reproducible; the payout audit split by
outcome; the singleton table recorded as an explicit deviation
(data-model.md's `rounds`/`roulette_rooms` sketches are superseded by
rounds-as-arithmetic for crash and roulette — crash had deviated
silently, 0029 records both); the rollback posture restated.

`docs/04-technical/data-model.md`

## 03:23 — milestone — Roulette live and verified; zero code defects, and the suite tripped over the design working

**What happened:** Roulette deployed and verified. Pre-deploy: harness
**58/58 first run** (derived-registry counts against independent
formulas, the exact-1/37 rejection arithmetic, the payout×coverage=36
identity per type, domain separation from crash under the shared
secret) and a clean splitter on 013. Production suite **37/37 on the
second pass** with the planner at full power: knowing every coming
pocket, it placed a straight ON the pocket (paid exactly 720 = 36×20),
one beside it (exactly 0), and the covering even-money type (exactly
40 = 2×20) — deterministic exact payouts, no luck involved. The pearl
fraction-carry was reproduced per-bet from a pre-settle snapshot in
`(round_index, bet_id)` order. Browser smoke: live phase, 13 bet
types, 36/37 and the rejection-recipe sentence all rendered.

**Why it matters:** Two things. First, **zero code defects** — the two
crash lessons (declared parameter order; rule phrases registered at
birth) were applied at build time and every rejection path returned
clean rule 400s on the first pass; the trend across nine verified
deployments reads 10 → 1 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 2 → 0. Second, the
first run's two failures were the suite tripping over **the design
working**: player 2's feed chip triggered placement's defensive
settle, which swept the straggler fixture the suite had parked —
"stragglers settle before a new stake" is exactly the promised
behavior, and the fixture forgot it. The restructured suite places
the straggler last and ages it six rounds, proving the time-based
response window surfaces results a round-count window would have
dropped. Check code fails like real code; this time it failed by
underestimating the code.
